== mutex-profile evidence (cpu=16, 1s run) ==
serveRedirect's OWN contribution to total captured mutex/lock delay, BEFORE the fix:
  5.91% cumulative (0.18s of 2.97s total) — and that 0.18s already includes downstream
  net/http.Redirect / net/url.Parse work, not just the RLock itself.
  >90% of total mutex/lock delay in this benchmark is GC/scheduler-internal locking
  (runtime.(*mheap).freeSpan / gcstopm / stopm / stopTheWorldWithSema), driven by the
  4 allocs/op that net/http.Redirect performs on every call — unrelated to m.mu and
  out of #247's scope.

CONCLUSION: the code-level goal (zero m.mu access anywhere in the request-dispatch path)
is fully and verifiably achieved (grep confirms no m.mu.RLock/Lock remains in ServeHTTP,
dispatch, or serveRedirect). The MEASURED ns/op impact on this synthetic benchmark is
statistically insignificant at cpu=4/16 (~, within noise) because Go's sync.RWMutex was
already near-lock-free for reader-only access with no concurrent writer (Use() runs once
at setup, before the benchmark loop) — it was never the dominant cost here.

== NO-REGRESSION check on root bench_test.go (order-flip control) ==
A first before/after sequential run of the root suite showed small but "significant"
apparent regressions on ParamRoute1/2/3 and WildcardRoute (+3-12%). Re-running with the
run ORDER REVERSED (after-code first, before-code second) flipped the sign of the same
deltas (ParamRoute1 now -7.46% "in favour of" whichever ran second) — conclusive proof
this is a run-order/thermal confound on the shared benchmark machine (which had just run
several minutes of continuous -race load), not a real effect of the mux.go change. No
code path reachable from ParamRoute1/2/3/WildcardRoute touches redirectMWPtr, the changed
Use()/serveRedirect() code, or reads any Mux field after the ones changed. See
root-before-clean.txt/root-after-clean.txt and after-first.txt/before-second.txt.

FINAL CALL: #247's AC ("ParallelRedirectTrailingSlash ns/op at cpu=16 improves vs
baseline") is NOT conclusively demonstrated on this benchmark (statistically "~", i.e. no
measurable difference either way) because the RWMutex was already cheap here — see the
mutex-profile evidence above. The "NO regression on root bench_test.go" half of the AC IS
satisfied (order-flip control shows the apparent deltas are measurement noise, not real).
